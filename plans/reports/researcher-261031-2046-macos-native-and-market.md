# Research: Native macOS Architecture and PDF Market Gap

Date: 2026-10-31 | Mode: read-only research | Scope: Part A (native macOS options for a Rust-engine document app) and Part B (Mac PDF market gaps)

## Summary

1. Recommendation: build a native Swift/AppKit macOS app that calls the existing Rust engine through UniFFI, uses PDFKit, Vision, App Intents and a Quick Look extension, and ships as a notarized Developer ID app with Sparkle 2.10.1 updates. Do not start with the Mac App Store.
2. Tauri 2 cannot deliver "truly native" on macOS. The upstream issues that matter (60fps on macOS, first-keystroke loss in WebView with third-party IMEs, fullscreen menu on borderless windows) are either closed as not planned or still open. Keep Tauri only as a fallback if Swift staffing is not available.
3. The Mac App Store is a poor fit for the current architecture. Guidelines 2.4.5 and 2.5.2 prohibit downloading additional code and third-party installers, and the repo's out-of-process engines (Pandoc, LibreOffice, the Docling pack) would need to be reworked or dropped for that channel.
4. Apple's on-device stack is capable enough for the product. Apple's support page lists Vietnamese among Apple Intelligence languages. Vision's accurate path appears to include Vietnamese, but that is from a secondary source and must be checked at runtime. Foundation Models requires macOS 26 or later and an Apple Intelligence-capable Mac.
5. The Mac market has a clear pattern of dated, first-hand complaints: paywalls added after purchase (PDF Expert), crashes and lost work (PDF Expert, PDFgear, Nitro), weak OCR and conversion coverage (PDFelement, UPDF), and cloud processing for "private" tools (iLovePDF, Smallpdf).
6. The strongest unmet needs are a one-time or non-escalating price, crash-safe editing, format-faithful conversion with a published fidelity benchmark, verifiable offline operation, and batch automation shared across GUI, CLI and MCP. Format-fidelity and Vietnamese-quality claims are hypotheses until tested.

## 0. Method, sources and grading

- Primary data was pulled directly: Apple developer documentation JSON endpoints, Apple support pages, GitHub and crates.io release metadata, and the App Store lookup and customer-review feeds. Those are the strongest evidence here.
- Secondary sources (blogs, aggregators, search summaries, one-author benchmarks) are labeled as such. Vendor claims are marked "vendor claim".
- Items I could not verify in this session are marked "unverified".
- Repository context used: `AGENTS.md` (conversion logic lives in `ariad-core`, `ariad-host` and `ariad-typst`; surfaces carry no conversion code; GPL tools run out of process; new apps need an `ARCHITECTURE.md` decision-log update). I did not read `ARCHITECTURE.md` itself.

### A2. Tauri 2 on macOS: achievable and not achievable

Facts, with source grade:

- Current CLI release: tauri-cli v2.12.4, published 2026-10-21 ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/tauri-apps/tauri/releases/latest)). Tauri is actively maintained.
- Frame rate: [tauri-apps/tauri#13978](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/13978) reports Tauri apps capped at 60fps on macOS, including ProMotion displays. It was closed as "not planned" one day after filing (2025-08-11). Treat the 60fps ceiling as unresolved upstream (maintainer grade: closed without a fix).
- Third-party IME: [tauri#15924](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/15924) (open, filed 2026-08-27) reports that the first keystroke is dropped with a third-party IME inside the WebView, while Safari with the same IME is unaffected. For a document editor with text fields, this is a serious issue.
- Borderless windows: [tauri#15823](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/15823) (open, 2026-08-04): the predefined fullscreen menu item does nothing on borderless macOS windows.
- Toolchain: [tauri#16386](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/16386) (open, 2026-10-23): macOS bundling fails on Xcode 27.1 with `actool`/`ibtoold`. This is a sign of macOS toolchain churn that the team will have to absorb.
- Launch on newer macOS: [tauri#15801](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/15801) reports the AMFI kernel check killing large binaries at launch on macOS 26.5+ because of 16KB page-size signing. Its current state was not checked.
- Memory: one author measured an idle Tauri app at about 81 MB main process plus about 159 MB across WebKit processes, versus 63 MB for a comparable SwiftUI app ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/johalputt/retrospective-6-months-of-using-tauri-20-for-desktop-apps-60-smaller-installers-20-more-13m4) and the related search results). Single-author, unverified benchmark.
- Vibrancy: the `window-vibrancy` crate is maintained inside the Tauri repo (PR #14470 bumps it to 0.7). The capability exists, but the visual quality was not verified.
- Text rendering and scroll physics: no authoritative source found. Apple forum comments about scroll smoothness concern NSScrollView, not Tauri, so they do not settle the question (unverified).

What Tauri can deliver: one codebase across Windows and Linux, native menus through predefined items, vibrancy via a crate, and reuse of the existing `apps/desktop` shell. What it cannot reliably deliver for a "truly native" macOS feel: 120Hz rendering, native text input behaviour across all IMEs, and AppKit-level menu and window behaviour in edge cases.

### A4. Distribution: Mac App Store, Developer ID and Sparkle

Mac App Store rules, quoted from the [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/):

- 2.4.5(i): apps "must be appropriately sandboxed".
- 2.4.5(ii): apps "must be packaged and submitted using technologies provided in Xcode; no third-party installers allowed. They must also be self-contained."
- 2.4.5(iv): apps "may not download or install standalone apps, kexts, additional code, or resources to add functionality or significantly change the app from what we see during the review process."
- 2.4.5(vii): apps "must use the Mac App Store to distribute updates; other update mechanisms are not allowed."
- 2.5.2: apps "may not ... download, install, or execute code which introduces or changes features or functionality of the app."

Consequences for this codebase:

- Sparkle is not allowed in the App Store build (2.4.5(vii)).
- Pandoc, LibreOffice and the Docling engine pack are separate executables or downloadable packs. Under 2.4.5(iv) and 2.5.2, the pack cannot be downloaded after review, and the executables would need to ship inside the bundle as sandboxed helpers.
- Embedding helper tools is possible. Apple's sandbox guide covers command-line tools built in Xcode or by an external build system ([Embedding a command-line tool in a sandboxed app](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/xcode/embedding-a-helper-tool-in-a-sandboxed-app.json)). An Apple DTS engineer's forum answer says a child process needs the `com.apple.security.inherit` entitlement, and that a binary cannot run both as a child and from the shell (secondary, [Apple forum thread 798947](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/798947)). Files opened by the parent need security-scoped bookmarks passed to the child (secondary, one blog).
- LibreOffice as an embedded helper is impractical in size and in review (inference, unverified). Pandoc's GPL licence also needs legal review for App Store distribution (unverified; the repo's `AGENTS.md` already requires GPL tools to run as separate processes).

Developer ID and notarization:

- Software distributed with Developer ID must be notarized to run on macOS 10.15 or later. The Mac App Store route does not need separate notarization because App Review performs equivalent checks ([Apple notarization doc](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/security/notarizing-macos-software-before-distribution.json)).
- This route allows Sparkle, downloadable engine packs and external helper binaries, which match the repo's current out-of-process design.

Sparkle:

- Latest release: Sparkle 2.10.1, published 2026-10-25 ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/sparkle-project/Sparkle/releases/latest)).
- Sandboxed apps need the Installer XPC service. The Downloader service is optional and only needed when the app does not hold the network-client entitlement ([Sparkle sandboxing](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/sandboxing/)). Sparkle's documentation does not address App Store distribution.

### A6. Recommendation

Choose option 1: a native Swift/AppKit macOS app over the Rust engine through UniFFI, shipped as a notarized Developer ID build with Sparkle 2.10.1. Use PDFKit for viewing, forms and annotations; Vision for on-device OCR; Foundation Models for on-device summaries and Q&A; App Intents for Shortcuts; a Quick Look preview extension for converted outputs. Keep the Rust engine as the only conversion implementation. Keep Tauri for non-macOS platforms if they are in scope.

Why this ranks first: it is the only option that meets "truly native" on macOS. It keeps conversion in one Rust codebase, which the repo's `AGENTS.md` already requires. Its distribution model matches the out-of-process engine design.

Main risks for the recommended option:

- Staffing: the team needs Swift and AppKit skills. Mitigation: start with a thin Swift shell over `ariad-host` and keep UI logic out of Rust.
- FFI churn: UniFFI minor releases have broken generated code. Mitigation: pin the version, add a Swift build and smoke test to CI, and read the changelog before each upgrade.
- Scope and governance: the repo rules say a new app must not contradict `ARCHITECTURE.md`, and any warranted change must update its decision log in the same change. A macOS app is a new surface, so that update is required. This was not checked against the file.
- Vietnamese OCR and summary quality are unverified. Test them on real Vietnamese documents before claiming them.

Fallback: if Swift staffing is unavailable, keep Tauri and treat the native-feel goal as unmet. Document the known macOS issues and set expectations.

Not recommended as a first channel: Mac App Store. It forbids the download-on-demand and external-helper model the engine uses. A later App Store build would need a reduced feature set (engines such as Pandoc, LibreOffice and Docling removed or re-architected).

### B2. Complaint themes (ranked by how often they recur across apps)

1. Subscription creep and paywalls after purchase (PDF Expert, UPDF, Nitro, PDFelement). Strongest evidence in this scan.
2. Crashes, freezes and lost work (PDF Expert, PDFgear, Nitro).
3. Text editing and conversion fidelity (PDFgear fonts, Nitro text editing, PDFelement page truncation). General tables and fonts issues are also reported in secondary guides.
4. OCR quality and speed (UPDF, PDFelement).
5. Watermarks and misleading "free" labels (PDFelement, UPDF).
6. Support and refunds (Nitro, UPDF).

### B3. Unmet needs for a privacy-first, offline converter and editor

Ranked by evidence strength. Each item notes what the product would need to prove.

1. Price that does not escalate after purchase. Evidence: PDF Expert's 2026 paywall complaint, UPDF's AI add-on complaints, Nitro's subscription-only Mac listing. This is a business decision rather than a technical one, and it is the easiest wedge to state. Prove it: publish the one-time price and never move features behind a new purchase.
2. Crash-safe editing that never loses work. Evidence: PDF Expert crash report (2026-10-16), PDFgear freeze on save (2026-10-28), Nitro freezes (2026-08-21). Engineering: autosave and versioned edits in the Rust engine, tested under kill-during-save. Prove it: a crash-recovery test suite.
3. Format-faithful PDF to DOCX and Markdown, with a published fidelity benchmark for tables and fonts. Evidence: PDFgear font mismatch, PDFelement first-page-only conversion, general table and font failures in secondary guides. No independent 2026 Mac benchmark was found (a gap in the evidence, not necessarily in the market). The repo already has a layout-aware path through the optional Docling pack (`AGENTS.md`). Prove it: publish a fixture-based benchmark with the fixture manifest the repo already requires.
4. Reliable on-device OCR with batch and folder processing. Evidence: UPDF's OCR complaint, PDFelement's slow OCR, Automator and Shortcuts folder workflows described as fragile (secondary). A hot-folder OCR app exists, so this is not wholly unmet (MacRumors thread on OnDevice OCR Pro, unverified details). Vision's accurate path is a candidate engine, with Vietnamese still to be confirmed.
5. Verifiable offline operation. Evidence: iLovePDF and Smallpdf process uploads server-side. Collate and LocalPDF claim on-device AI, but those claims are unverified. PDF Expert's AI privacy behaviour is not documented. Prove it: a documented "no network" test mode, verified in CI with networking disabled. Under the sandbox, an app without the network-client entitlement cannot make network connections (inference, unverified against Apple's current docs).
6. Grounded on-device document Q&A with citations. Evidence: UPDF's 2026 complaint that uploaded files are not used in the answer. Foundation Models supports on-device generation and guided output, with the caveat that it has a limited context window (`contextSize`). Prove it: answers cite page numbers, and tests check that answers change when the document changes.
7. Batch conversion and automation shared across GUI, CLI and MCP. Evidence: PDFelement handles only the first page in one reported case, Automator and Shortcuts folder flows are described as fragile, and the repo already exposes `ashift` CLI and `ashift mcp` surfaces (`AGENTS.md`). The differentiator is one engine across three surfaces. Prove it: the same fixture produces identical output through GUI, CLI and MCP.
8. Vietnamese-language document quality. Evidence: Apple Intelligence supports Vietnamese (official). Vision's accurate path appears to include Vietnamese (secondary, verify at runtime). Competitor Vietnamese quality was not tested. This is a hypothesis for a product aimed at Vietnamese users. Prove it: a Vietnamese OCR and summary test set with measured accuracy.

## Unresolved questions and limitations

- Vietnamese on Vision's accurate path with revision 3 on macOS 27 is unverified. Confirm with `supportedRecognitionLanguages` on a device.
- Foundation Models `supportedLanguages` for Vietnamese on the target OS is unverified. Apple's doc says the model supports the languages Apple Intelligence supports, but the exact list was not queried.
- Apple's pages for Share, Action and Finder Sync extensions, Services and Spotlight importers did not load. Their current constraints are unverified.
- Apple's notarization and sandbox rules were read from Apple doc JSON and guidelines. The App Store review treatment of GPL helper binaries such as Pandoc, and whether LibreOffice could be embedded at all, were not researched and need legal review.
- The Mac App Store helper-tool details rely partly on an Apple forum answer (secondary). Confirm against current Xcode documentation before implementation.
- Tauri issue #15801 (AMFI launch kill) and #16386 (Xcode 27.1 bundling) were not checked for current state beyond the list view.
- Official Adobe, Nitro, PDFelement and UPDF prices were not retrieved. The pricing figures above are secondary or App Store data, and regional variants conflict.
- Customer-review samples are the most recent 50 per app. They show the kinds of complaints, not their frequency.
- The repo's `ARCHITECTURE.md` was not read. The architecture verdict assumes no existing decision forbids a second macOS surface beyond what `AGENTS.md` states.
- No benchmarks were run. Performance claims (scroll, memory, launch time) are inference unless marked otherwise.
- Vietnamese market size and Vietnamese user demand were not researched.
